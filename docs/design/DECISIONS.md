| — | Presentation architecture (binding, added 2026-07-28) | **The tile simulation is the model; every camera is a view over it.** Eli, emphatic and unprompted: *"do not ditch the tile sim... we need the tile sim in the background even if the player playing through it sees something 1st person."* Long-range target register (POST-simulation, not current work): *"Morrowind skills and systems with FO New Vegas factions with Daggerfall's graphics"* + *"faked 3D via clever use of 2D as Gabri sleuths about"* — where "Daggerfall's graphics" means its **billboarded sprites for everything alive**, NOT its raycast geometry. Why this is architecturally cheap rather than a rewrite: ArchUnit already forbids `sim-core` importing libGDX/`client-observer`, so the sim does not know it is being drawn; `client-observer` is *a* client, not *the* client, and a first-person combat screen (or a later exploration camera) is another view over the same deterministic tick stream — two views can never disagree, because the sim is the single source of truth. Everything load-bearing is grid logic a camera cannot disturb: 1-occupant-per-cell, A* pathing, z-links, sense radii, work reach (chebyshev 2), the S7 corridor-pinch predicate. `Actor.facing()` already exists (it prints on the character sheet) and Play mode already tracks a played actor, so a soul's-eye camera is largely position + facing. Depth Vision (S4, extended to actors in S5-6) already renders lower z-bands with scaled darkening and cool haze — a real working depth cue, and most of the way to convincing pseudo-3D with no polygons. **Rules that bind any future camera work:** (1) it is a NEW CLIENT MODULE or a client-side screen, NEVER a sim change; (2) the headless tile view stays the verification source of truth, so no rendered scene can quietly disagree with the sim; (3) it must not touch the S8-S12 economy arc — you cannot investigate an economy with no secrets in it, so the world that generates the mysteries gets built first. |
| — | First-person EXPLORATION camera (added 2026-07-29) | Eli, verbatim: *"I want a demo with the ability to switch between 1st person and the top-down tile view. I'm again envisioning something similar to Daggerfall where you can walk around and interact with the environment. It can still be tile-based movement and combat will still be in a battle screen eventually. Make sure you don't make it jarring, interpolate when moving so that you get a really smooth high fps if you need to. Like how in the Doom game the whole thing is technically 2D top down with trickery. I want that, but also with the ability to have a Z-axis like Daggerfall."* Ruled a **keepable foundation**, not a spike. **This does not touch 2b**: combat remains a separate paused first-person screen entered on encounter contact, and none of it is built here. Lives entirely in `com.trojia.client.fpv` + `FirstPersonInput`; `sim-core` untouched. **The technique**, chosen and defended in `FirstPersonPlanner`'s javadoc: a per-cell perspective projection with painter-ordered quads and billboards — 2D trickery in the Doom sense (no depth buffer, no mesh, no matrix stack; every surface is a textured quad in the existing `SpriteBatch`) but with a real pinhole projection instead of a column raycast. A Wolf3D/Doom raycaster was rejected because Doom could not put a room over a room and this ward is three walk planes with roof slums above and a flooded harbour below, and because its floors are a per-pixel pass no headless test can read; real 3D was rejected because visibility would live in the GPU. Accepted costs, all written down where they bite: no true pitch (looking up is a horizon shear), affine texture mapping across a quad, and one plan-view region on every wall face until an art pack authors a `face` form token. **Rulings the tile data could not make for itself, all pure rendering constants in `BandGeometry` with no sim meaning:** a z-band is **2.75 tile-widths** tall (a tile is one shoulder of standing room; a storey is about 2.75 of them — setting the band to one tile-width is the crawlspace failure this view is most often fudged into), eye height 1.70, a fluid surface sits `depth/8` up its own band (which puts full-depth harbour water just under the lip of the quay and a bathhouse pool ankle-deep, from one rule), and an unroofed column resolves to a **flat ambient-lit sky band** rather than to the art pack's void black, which read as a bug. **Binding on this and any later camera:** the movement seam is `PlayModeInput.applyMovement` and there is no second route into the sim's move intent; the view yaw is client-side and `Actor.setFacing` is never called; the vertical reach and the depth shade are `DepthVision`'s own, so the two views cannot disagree about how far down a column you can see or how recessed it looks; and the whole visibility decision is a GL-free planner returning records that name the cell each came from, so `FirstPersonAgreementTest` can stand on the real baked ward and diff the two views. **AMENDED 2026-07-31 — movement is CONTINUOUS, not tile-stepped.** After a frame-by-frame study of Barony (see `COMBAT-FEEL-REFERENCE.md`), Eli ruled verbatim: *"I want the continuous movement of Barony rather than Grimrock style."* This supersedes *"It can still be tile-based movement"* in the brief quoted above; nothing else in this row changes. **The binding consequence:** sim-core forbids float/double in state, and that rule is what makes the world hash cross-platform, so continuous movement is **sub-tile FIXED-POINT** — `(tile << 8) \| sub` in Q8, 256 steps across a tile — never floating point. It is specifically NOT solved by making the player's position client-only float state: that removes the player from the simulation, and the first time combat, occupancy, shoving or line-of-sight needs to know exactly where the player stands, the authoritative answer would live in the renderer. NPCs stay tile-stepped and interpolated at draw time (the existing stride-easing path); only the player takes sub-tile authority at first, and widening it later is a resolution change rather than an architecture change. The `STEP_ARRIVAL_FRACTION` / `strideEase` machinery exists to smooth discrete tile steps and is **replaced, not retuned**, for the player. Measured reference: Barony's keyboard turn is ~60-70 deg/s against our current 165 deg/s. **AMENDED 2026-08-04 — the C++ build shipped the crawlspace failure this row warned about, and the fix rounds 2.75 to 3.** `granadad`'s renderer drew a z-band as `z` to `z + 1.0` in the same units it used for a tile's width, with the eye at 205/256 = 0.80 of that: exactly the "one tile-width" band named above as the failure mode. Eli, playing it, verbatim: *"Definitely fix the roofs, all buildings only appear to be one tile high, I'd triple the amount of vertical height you're treating as '1 floor'."* **The number is now `kTilesPerBand = 3` in `native/include/granadad/sim/vertical_scale.hpp`**, with eye height 1.70 tiles unchanged from this row. Three rather than 2.75 for one reason that is specific to this codebase and does not apply to the Java one: the C++ body's vertical axis is Q8 integer and no rounding is allowed anywhere in it, and 3 is the only value in range for which every conversion is exact — a band is 768 Q8 and the eye's 435 Q8 divides by 3 with no remainder, where 2.75 gives 704 and a repeating 158.18. **Nobody should "restore" 2.75 later**: the two numbers are the same ruling and the difference is under the precision anybody can see. |

| — | Trojian tenure: the charge and the bond | (Eli, 2026-07-29) **Property in Granadad is a three-tier ground lease under a Church freehold, and Trojian serfdom is the debt that settles it — a relation between two actors with terms, not a caste and not a status flag.** Eli, verbatim: *"In lieu of paying Royals for rent you can lease yourself to your Den Duke (update lore to include this term, a Den Duke is someone who owns and rents property in Granadad.) This is also the same sort of system that a Craftlord might use to get workers in his shop, and how Guilds acquire competition."* Then, on the structure: *"The den dukes should own a parent plot that contains all houses within the compound. Den dukes own the compound while individuals own the houses."* Then, superseding any two-tier reading of the first: *"The plots should be owned by the priests of the flame for historical and legal preservation. Then dukes and tenants can make agreements to lease. A priest should be required to evict and they should consider the tenant too."* **THE THREE TIERS.** (1) The **Priests of the Flame** hold every PLOT in trust, for historical and legal preservation. The ground is not property; it is a **charge**, and it is never sold, only let. (2) A **DEN DUKE** holds the charge on a plot — the compound, its wall, its gate, its courtyard — pays the Flame a charge-rent, and lets the ground under each house to that house's owner. A **CRAFTLORD** is the identical instrument on working ground (a yard, a shed, a quay frontage): same table, different title. (3) **Individuals own their HOUSES outright**, standing on ground they will never own. A family can own its house free and clear and still owe the man who rents the earth beneath it, and that man does not own the earth either. **RENT IS GROUND RENT** — the *ground penny*, owed by each house-owner to the plot's Den Duke, never rent on the dwelling. **PAYABLE IN ROYALS OR IN YOURSELF:** a house-owner who cannot find the penny may go **bondsworn**, leasing their own labour to their Den Duke until the arrears are worked off. That is what Trojian serfdom is. A serf is not born a serf; a serf is a house-owner who had a bad quarter. **ROOFTOP RULING:** a rooftop lodger rents from the HOUSE-OWNER BENEATH THEM, not from the Den Duke — a roof is part of a house, and the house is individually owned. This makes every mansion-poor house-owner a petty landlord, and it means that when a house is taken for arrears the roof goes with it and the roof people were never parties to the case. The priest weighs the tenant; nobody weighs the lodgers. **EVICTION REQUIRES A PRIEST, AND THE PRIEST IS AN ARBITER.** A Den Duke cannot turn a family out; he must bring a **petition** to the Flame and the priest weighs the tenant as well as the arrears (see DOCKS-GAZETTEER §2.8 for the hearing, its inputs and its six outcomes — the outcomes are not yes and no). This completes a pattern already ruled: the Church is the ward's purse, approves trades as ordinary business, and sanctions the redemption of a scalp. One conscience now sits under trade, under blood money, and under the roof over every family's head. The Church never opposes anyone openly; it makes itself unavoidable while appearing to serve. **RETIRES A PLACEHOLDER:** DOCKS-GAZETTEER §4.2's SERF dossier framing ("'serf' framing placeholder") is superseded — the ward's serf mass is now defined by a MECHANISM (a debt relation dressed as a tenancy) rather than a caste label. This is a ruling, not a drift. **BINDING ON PRESENTATION:** ownership is never rendered. Eli, verbatim: *"no it doesn't need to be visible, just use the info to populate signage appropriately."* No ownership overlay, no painted ground, no boundary lines, no debug layer. Ownership is a fact the world knows and the SIGNS report; the player learns who holds the ground by reading the ward. |

| — | The brawl line (added 2026-08-04, S2) | **A fight resolves IN THE WORLD, with no screen transition, exactly while all three of these hold:** (B1) **nothing edged is out** — every hand in it holds fists, something improvised off a table, or a carried cudgel; a knife, a boat-hook or a cutlass is not a brawl weapon; (B2) **nobody means to kill** — intent is state, not a guess, and an actor who escalates to `Kill` has decided; (B3) **nobody is being finished** — a fighter at or under a quarter health is BLOODIED, and beating a bloodied man while meaning him `Harm` is not a bar fight any more, whatever is in your hands. The moment any one of them stops holding the fight is LETHAL and belongs to the dedicated first-person combat screen (2b). **B3 is what makes this a rule rather than an inventory check:** two men swinging stools at each other is a brawl however long it goes on, as long as neither has decided to do more than win — a Subdue beating that puts somebody on the floor is exactly what a bouncer does for a living. What tips it is intent meeting damage. **Corollary, ruled at the same time:** a brawl never kills the player; hit points floor at 1, the fight ends there and what follows is being carried out, not more fighting.
